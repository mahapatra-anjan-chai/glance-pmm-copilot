6 Design Principles
PRINCIPLE 01

One Visual System, Every Cover

Pick one cover template and never deviate. Same dark base, same type placement, same color language. Variety kills credibility in magazine layout.

Same dark base every coverOne type styleDifferent styles per postMixed photo types
PRINCIPLE 02

Covers Are Editorial, Not Marketing

Marketing covers say "look at our product." Editorial covers say "here's a world worth entering." For B2B readers, a marketing cover is a trust killer before they read a word.

Abstract, conceptual imageryDark, minimalProduct screenshotsGlance logo dominating
PRINCIPLE 03

Titles: 5–7 Words Max

Long titles overflow the grid card and break the layout rhythm. The discipline Glance needs: "The Search Bar is Dead" — not "Why the Traditional Search Bar is Becoming Obsolete in an AI-First Commerce World." One is a headline. The other is a sentence.

"The Commerce Layer Nobody Sees""What Your Agent Knows About You"Long descriptive titles with colons
PRINCIPLE 04

Subtitles Do the Explaining

Title = hook. Subtitle = context. For Glance: title "The Search Bar is Dead," subtitle "Why AI agents have made the most overused UI element in history redundant." The title creates intrigue; the subtitle delivers on it. Both fields must be filled every time.

Subtitle under 18 wordsTitle = mystery, subtitle = payoffSubtitle that repeats the titleBlank subtitle
PRINCIPLE 05

Dark Background = Intelligence Signal

Deep navy/charcoal covers signal seriousness to an industry reader the same way FT pink does. The cover color is positioning before the first word.

Deep navy base (#0D1520)Amber/teal/blue accents onlyWhite or pastel backgroundsBright gradients
PRINCIPLE 06

Watermark on Every Cover

Every cover shared on LinkedIn, in WhatsApp, or embedded in a press article should carry the Glance identity. Add "by Glance" in DM Mono, bottom-left, 40% opacity. Small enough not to dominate — present enough that every image that escapes the page still points back to Glance.

Small "by Glance" bottom-leftConsistent position every timeFull logo dominating the coverNo attribution at all

Brand Page Setup

Configure the page as an SEO and credibility surface — not a newsletter product.

STEP 1
Name and URL — optimise for search, not just branding
The publication name shows in Google snippets
+
  • Publication name: Consider "Agentic Commerce Intelligence — by Glance" over just "Glance." Descriptive names carry keyword weight in search snippets.
  • URL: Aim for commerce.glance.com via CNAME, or at minimum claim a short, clean Substack subdomain.
  • Description field: Write with your primary keyword in the first sentence. This appears as the Google meta description.
Substack descriptions appear as meta descriptions in Google results. A keyword-rich description improves click-through on search impressions.
STEP 2
About Page — write it as a credibility document
Often the first thing a journalist, investor, or brand partner reads
+
  • Para 1: Define "agentic commerce" in Glance's words. Own the definition. This framing gets quoted and linked to.
  • Para 2: Who publishes this and why Glance is uniquely positioned (scale, data, product depth, device layer).
  • Para 3: Who reads this and what they get. Speak directly to both audiences: industry and tech-savvy consumers.
  • Do NOT make this a marketing page. Write it as editorial credentials, not a product pitch.
When journalists research agentic commerce, they often read the About page of publications covering the space. Make this page something a reporter would cite as a source.
STEP 3
Enable Notes and Recommendations
The two highest-ROI organic growth features on Substack
+
  • Notes: Settings → Notes. Post 2–3 per week. Appears in feeds of non-subscribers. This is the primary discovery mechanism without a newsletter.
  • Recommendations: Settings → Growth. When new subscribers sign up, they're shown publications you recommend. Set up 5–8 reciprocal recommendations with adjacent newsletters.
Recommendations is the single most underused growth lever on Substack. One recommendation from a newsletter with 10K subscribers can send 300–500 new subscribers in a week.

Publishing Cadence

How often to publish — optimised for SEO indexing and content quality, not email delivery windows.

The rule of thumb: Consistent frequency signals topical authority to Google. Publishing 2 quality posts per week consistently beats publishing 5 average posts then going quiet. Choose the cadence you can sustain at quality.

Monthly

1× / month
  • Lowest commitment
  • Hardest to build momentum
  • Works for a team of 1
  • Long-form deep dives only

Bi-Weekly

2× / month
  • Sustainable for small team
  • Enough to stay top-of-mind
  • Mix of long + short formats
  • Best starting cadence for Glance

Weekly

4× / month
  • Maximum growth velocity
  • Requires dedicated writer + editor
  • High quality-drop risk without process
  • Unlock after 3 months of bi-weekly

Activate in Phase 2 only

Guest editors on a publishing platform (vs. a newsletter) have a different value proposition: they bring their own audience to a post that permanently lives on Glance's domain — contributing to topical authority, not just filling an inbox. The SEO value of a well-credentialed guest byline is real and lasting.

🎯

Who to Invite

Industry voices whose audience overlaps with Glance's target readers: commerce execs, AI researchers, retail analysts. Their byline = credibility + backlink from their own promotion.

📄

Brief Essentials

Topic, target keyword, word count, deadline, approval rounds, and what the guest gets: byline, bio, link, co-promotion on Notes and LinkedIn from the Glance account.

🔗

SEO Value of Guest Posts

When the guest promotes the post to their network, inbound links accumulate on Glance's domain — not theirs. The publishing platform model means Glance captures all SEO authority from external promotion.

⚖️

Editorial Control

Glance maintains final edit rights on all guest content. Ensure every piece uses the right category language, links to cornerstone content, and meets publishing standards.
