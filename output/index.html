American Express Launches ACE Developer Kit with Industry-First AI Agent Purchase Protection
Digital Commerce 360 / American Express Newsroom ↗
American Express debuted its Agentic Commerce Experiences (ACE) developer kit, providing a five-component framework — agent registration, account enablement, intent intelligence, payment credentials, and cart context — that allows developers to embed Amex cards directly into AI-driven transactions with full tokenized security. Alongside the kit, Amex introduced Amex Agent Purchase Protection, an industry-first commitment to cover card member purchases made by registered AI agents, removing a key trust barrier that has slowed mainstream adoption of autonomous checkout.
The ACE 'intent intelligence' component — where the agent signals purchase intent before checkout — maps precisely to Glance's D0 Orchestrator model, which already aggregates intent signals from five parallel sub-agents (Weather, Trends, Occasion, Physical Stylist, Lifestyle Detective). Glance could expose an ACE-compatible intent feed that lets Amex-registered agents seamlessly complete purchases surfaced on the lockscreen, creating a trust-sealed ambient checkout loop no other discovery surface can replicate.

Amazon vs. Perplexity Injunction Sets Legal Precedent for Who Controls AI Agent Access to Retail Platforms
GeekWire / PYMNTS ↗
A federal judge granted Amazon a preliminary injunction blocking Perplexity's Comet AI browser from accessing password-protected Amazon pages on behalf of customers, ruling that user permission does not equal platform authorization under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. As of May 2026, Perplexity's April 1 appeal remains active before the Ninth Circuit, with the Software & Information Industry Association filing an amicus brief supporting Amazon — a decision that could define whether retailers can unilaterally wall off third-party shopping agents and reshape the entire agentic commerce ecosystem.
The Amazon ruling exposes the fragility of third-party agent models that must negotiate — or litigate — access to every retailer's platform. Glance's approach is structurally different: brands integrate directly into the D0 feed as first-party Brand AI Store partners, meaning no scraping, no unauthorized access, and no legal exposure. As the court battle chills third-party agent growth, Glance's consent-first brand partnership model becomes a governance-safe alternative for retailers who want agentic discovery without the CFAA risk.

🕳 Whitespace Angles — Zero coverage in past 28 days

  • Pre-intent commerce: surfacing products before users form a query
    Every major publication covers agentic commerce as pull-based — user opens ChatGPT/Perplexity and asks. Nobody has published the counterargument that the most powerful commerce surface is pre-intent: before the user decides to shop. Glance's D0 feed operates in this window at scale across 175M devices.
    Glance is the only company that can write this with production data — D0 impressions, D0-to-tap rate, D0-to-checkout latency. These metrics don't exist in any other platform's reporting because no other platform operates in the pre-intent window.
  • OEM-level AI as a trust layer that app stores cannot replicate
    Stratechery and The Agentic Commerce Frontier both cover UCP and ACP protocols (Google/OpenAI respectively) but treat OEM-embedded surfaces as an afterthought. No one has written about OEM pre-consent — how Glance bypasses app-install friction by being baked into the OS layer at the factory.
    Glance's consent architecture is negotiated once with the OEM at device manufacture. The agent on the lockscreen never has to ask the user for permission to run — because the permission framework is colocated at the OS layer. No app-based platform can replicate this without a separate, user-blocking permission flow.
  • The D0 activation window as a measurable commerce category
    Commerce content universally frames the funnel as starting at search or app-open. The window between device wake and first app-open (D0) is unaddressed in any major publication. Glance can coin and own this category with device-scale data.
    Glance runs production agents in the D0 window — Weather, Trends, Occasion, Physical Stylist, Lifestyle Detective — with measurable output at each step. The D0 funnel can be instrumented and published in a way no other platform can match because no other platform is operating there.
  • Multi-agent orchestration for passive personalisation (vs. active instruction)
    Coverage of multi-agent systems focuses on task automation (book a flight, buy a product on command). The Glance architecture — 5 passive sub-agents (Weather, Trends, Occasion, Physical Stylist, Lifestyle Detective) that run without user instruction — is architecturally distinct and uncovered.
    The Orchestrator's reconciliation logic — merging contradictory outputs from 5 sub-agents into a coherent lockscreen card within a 200ms render budget — is genuinely novel. No multi-agent framework paper has addressed passive inference at production speed as a commerce problem.
  • What UCP and ACP miss: commerce without a session
    Both Google's Universal Commerce Protocol and OpenAI's Agentic Commerce Protocol assume a user-initiated session. Lockscreen commerce has no session — only ambient signal. Glance is the only scaled platform that can write this counterpoint with real data.
    The structural gap in every agentic protocol published in 2026: they all start at T+1 (user opens app or chat). Glance operates at T+0 — device wake, no session, no query. A Stratechery-style framework piece from Glance on 'commerce without a session' would be cited by every protocol designer in the field.
  • Virtual try-on as infrastructure, not a feature
    Google's Doppl shutdown and VTON integration into Search has been covered as a feature story. Nobody has written the infrastructure argument: that VTON is a conversion layer that belongs to whoever controls the surface seen first. Glance surfaces it on the lockscreen before Search.
    Glance's Physical Stylist sub-agent delivers zero-upload VTON — inferred body type and style preferences from device signals, rendered by Gemini + Imagen. Google's VTON requires a selfie and an open browser. The infrastructure gap is real and publishable with a side-by-side friction analysis.
