HBR Research: Scarcity Timers and Discounts Fail on AI Shopping Agents — Only Ratings and Price Work
Harvard Business Review ↗
A peer-reviewed study by Jafar Sabbah and Oguz Acar, published in HBR May 2026, tested 8 classic e-commerce persuasion tactics (scarcity, countdown timers, strike-through pricing, vouchers, bundles) across GPT-4.1-mini, GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite across thousands of simulated shopping rounds — finding that only star ratings and raw price reliably shifted agent selection, while all other tactics produced model-specific noise or backfired. Advanced reasoning models actively penalized overt persuasion signals.
Glance's feed is already agent-mediated: the D0 Orchestrator synthesizes sub-agent signals, not human psychology cues. This research validates Glance's architecture — curating products through contextual relevance (occasion, weather, lifestyle) rather than manufactured urgency. As brand budgets shift toward 'agent-optimized' content, Glance's contextual signal stack (5 parallel sub-agents) becomes the most defensible placement in commerce media.

  • OpenAI paused Instant Checkout — what the trust gap means for ambient commerce
    OpenAI quietly paused its Instant Checkout feature in early May 2026. Coverage treats this as a trust calibration problem. Nobody has written the counter-angle: the D0 lockscreen card shows the user the product before any tap is required — it is inherently a higher-trust surface than a chatbot completing checkout in the background.
    The structural reason Instant Checkout failed: users couldn't see what was being purchased until it was done. The D0 lockscreen inverts this — product is visible before any action is taken. Glance can publish the 'trust architecture' comparison that explains why ambient surfaces have higher inherent trust than autonomous background agents.
  • UCP coalition now includes Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Salesforce — and still no OEM layer
    The Universal Commerce Protocol tech council added Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, and Salesforce this week alongside Google, Shopify, and Wayfair. Every major platform is now in UCP — except OEM-embedded surfaces. The coalition covers every app layer but misses the device layer entirely. Glance operates at the device layer and requires no protocol membership to surface products.
    The UCP coalition is the most powerful signal yet that the app-layer agentic commerce stack is consolidating — and that the only uncovered layer is the OEM layer where Glance operates. A Stratechery-style analysis of what the coalition misses would be cited across the entire commerce press cycle.
  • FIDO Agent Trust frameworks vs. OEM pre-consent — why the registration layer is unnecessary on Glance
    FIDO and Experian are building 'Agent Trust' frameworks that require agents to register, get credentials, and prove identity before acting on behalf of users. Glance's OEM pre-consent model is more fundamental: Glance is baked into the device at manufacture, with consent negotiated once at the OS level. No registration needed.
    The trust frameworks everyone is building solve a problem that the OEM layer doesn't have. Glance can publish the architectural comparison — delegated trust (FIDO) vs. ambient consent (OEM pre-install) — that clarifies why the app-layer trust problem is structurally different from the device-layer trust model.

    Multiple newsletters are bullish on stablecoins as the native settlement layer for machine-to-machine commerce. The argument is that card networks are too slow and expensive for micro-agent transactions. Glance's lockscreen commerce operates over Stripe/UPI rails with human-initiated tap-to-buy — no machine-to-machine settlement needed.
    This is a non-problem for D0 surfaces. Publishing the contrast — why stablecoin settlement is irrelevant when the human is still in the loop at device level — positions Glance as architecturally simpler and safer than the fully autonomous agent stack the fintech press is building toward.
