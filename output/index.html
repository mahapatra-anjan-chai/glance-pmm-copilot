🕳 Whitespace Angles — Zero coverage in past 28 days

  • The OEM Trust Layer: Why Pre-Installed AI Commerce Beats App-Downloaded Commerce
    Every newsletter covering agentic commerce assumes discovery happens through ChatGPT, Google UCP, or a merchant's own app. Nobody is writing about the trust premium that comes from OEM-level pre-installation — the fact that a Samsung or Motorola ships with Glance already on it creates a fundamentally different consent model than a downloaded app.
    Glance sits on 175M devices not through a download funnel but through OEM agreements. Commerce consent is granted at device setup — Day 0 — before any brand has advertised. This is the trust architecture the entire agentic commerce industry is trying to reverse-engineer through payment mandates that Glance already has by default.
  • D0 Commerce: The Monetization Window Nobody Knows Exists
    The 28-day corpus has extensive coverage of AI discovery (ChatGPT Shopping, UCP), payment rails, and post-purchase loyalty. Zero posts address Day 0 — the device activation moment when a new phone owner's preferences are uncontested. This is arguably the highest-intent discovery window in mobile commerce.
    Glance's lockscreen is live at device setup — before any competing app is downloaded, before a browser preference is established. D0 is Glance's unfair advantage: a clean slate with massive intent.
  • VTON as Conversion Infrastructure, Not a Novelty Feature
    The corpus mentions virtual try-on briefly. But nobody has written the infrastructure argument: VTON is what turns a passive lockscreen content viewer into an active buyer. The mechanism — see outfit on lock screen, tap to see it on your body, buy — is unaddressed in any newsletter.
    Glance's selfie-driven VTON closes the loop between ambient discovery and transactional intent in a single gesture. With AI-referred visitors converting 42% better than non-AI (Adobe Q1 2026 data), a VTON layer compounds further.
  • Zapps: The Mini-App Layer That Makes the Lock Screen a Platform
    Discussions of agentic commerce UI focus on chatbots or conversational checkout flows. The concept of lightweight, embeddable mini-apps (Zapps) surfaced directly on the lock screen — no app launch, no context switch — is entirely absent from the discourse.
    Zapps are the zero-friction answer to the Toggle Tax. Instead of switching from lockscreen to browser to cart to payment app, Zapps collapse that stack to a single interaction layer. Glance can write the definitive piece on why mini-app-on-lockscreen is the mobile-native equivalent of UCP.
  • The Global South Commerce Stack: Why India-First AI Commerce Architecture Wins Emerging Markets
    The agentic commerce corpus is overwhelmingly US/EU-centric. Asia-Pacific gets a paragraph. Nobody is writing about the India-specific architecture decisions — high mobile-first penetration, UPI rails, prepaid device economics — that make lockscreen commerce viable at scale.
    Glance built its 175M device base primarily across India and Southeast Asia, markets where the lock screen is often the only 'browser' a user interacts with. The infrastructure decisions that work in those markets are ahead of Western agentic commerce, not derivative of it.
  • Ambient Commerce vs. Intent-Driven Commerce: The Attention Architecture Nobody Is Mapping
    All 28-day coverage treats commerce as intent-first: a user decides to shop, opens an app, and an AI helps execute. Zero newsletters explore commerce that begins with ambient exposure — the idle-state glance at a lockscreen — and converts through progressive engagement rather than initiated search.
    The lockscreen is the only surface in commerce that earns attention without soliciting it. Glance can publish a framework distinguishing 'ambient discovery funnels' (impression → tap → try-on → buy) from 'intent execution funnels' (search → compare → checkout).
  • The Machine-Readability Gap: Merchants Are Invisible to AI Agents — But Visible to Lockscreens
    Product pages average only 66/100 machine-readability scores — merchants have not structured data for agents. Nobody has pointed out that the lockscreen bypasses this problem: Glance ingests merchant catalog data directly through brand partnerships, not by scraping HTML.
    While the industry debates robots.txt configuration and UCP schema compliance, Glance's 400+ brand partnerships deliver structured product data through direct integration. Post title: 'Why Your Brand Is Invisible to AI Agents — But Not to 175M Lockscreens.'
  • The Sponsored Prompt Problem: Native Lockscreen Commerce Has Better Trust Signals Than Chat Ads
    Topsort's Sponsored Prompts data shows 75% of consumers would trust AI recommendations less if they knew they were sponsored. No newsletter has proposed an alternative trust model — only flagged the problem.
    Glance's lockscreen content is editorially curated and VTON-driven rather than keyword-bid-driven, creating a disclosure model where 'this brand is a Glance partner' carries less stigma than 'this chatbot recommendation was paid for.'
